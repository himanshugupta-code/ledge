Taking a screenshot on a Mac is instant. Getting it into the app where you actually need it, a Slack thread, an email or a bug report, is where the time goes. Here are three ways to skip the file-hunting step.

## 1. Drag the floating thumbnail

After you take a screenshot, macOS shows a small thumbnail in the bottom-right corner of the screen for a few seconds.

- **Drag the thumbnail** straight into a message, email, document or Finder window. The screenshot goes where you drop it.
- **Click it** to open Markup, where you can crop, draw and add text before sharing.
- **Swipe it to the right** to save it immediately and dismiss the thumbnail.

The thumbnail only stays for a few seconds, which is great when you are sending right away and frustrating when you are not. If it gets in your way, you can turn it off: press `Shift` `Cmd` `5`, click **Options** and untick **Show Floating Thumbnail**. Screenshots then save immediately.

## 2. Copy to the clipboard instead

If you know you will paste the screenshot rather than keep it, add `Control` to the shortcut:

| Shortcut | What it copies |
| --- | --- |
| `Control` `Shift` `Cmd` `3` | The whole screen |
| `Control` `Shift` `Cmd` `4` | A selection you drag |
| `Control` `Shift` `Cmd` `4`, then `Space` | A single window |

Then paste with `Cmd` `V` in any app that accepts images. Nothing is saved to disk, so there is nothing to clean up later. The downside: you only get one screenshot at a time, and the next copy replaces it.

## 3. Keep recent screenshots on a shelf

The thumbnail disappears after a few seconds and the clipboard holds one image. When you take several screenshots and share them over the next half hour, neither works well.

[Ledge](/en/) keeps your most recent screenshots on a shelf at the top of your screen:

1. Take screenshots the usual way. Each one lands on the shelf.
2. When you need one, rest the pointer at the top edge of the screen, or press `Cmd` `Option` `L`.
3. **Drag** a screenshot into Messages, Mail, Slack, a document or a Finder window. Or use its copy button, or click it to annotate first.

Because the shelf keeps your recent captures, you can grab the screenshot you took twenty minutes ago as easily as the one you just took. Ledge is free, open source, and works offline.

## Which one should you use?

- Sharing one screenshot right now: **drag the thumbnail**.
- Pasting into a chat and never needing the file: **copy to the clipboard**.
- Taking several screenshots and sharing them over time: **use a shelf like Ledge**.
