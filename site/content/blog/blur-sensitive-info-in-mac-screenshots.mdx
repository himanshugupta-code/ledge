You take a screenshot to show a bug, a setting, or a result. Then you paste it into a chat, a ticket or a public issue, and only afterwards notice the API key in the corner, a customer's email in the table, or a notification banner from a private conversation.

Screenshots are the easiest way to leak something by accident. This guide covers what to look for and a quick way to hide it on a Mac.

## What to look for before you share

Scan the whole image, not just the part you meant to show:

- **Secrets:** API keys, tokens, passwords, connection strings, recovery codes.
- **Personal details:** names, email addresses, phone numbers, addresses, order numbers.
- **Background clutter:** notification banners, other browser tabs, the menu bar, file paths that include your username.
- **Faces and photos** of people who have not agreed to be shared.

If you are not sure whether something is sensitive, hide it. Nobody complains about an extra blur.

## Why a plain screenshot is not enough

macOS makes taking a screenshot easy (`Cmd` `Shift` `3` for the screen, `Cmd` `Shift` `4` for a selection). It is less helpful once the image is on your Desktop. The built-in Markup tools in Preview and Quick Look can draw shapes, arrows and text, but they do not give you a dedicated blur or pixelate tool. That pushes many people toward covering text with a box by hand, or opening a full image editor for a ten-second job.

## Three ways to hide information

1. **Cover it with a solid shape.** A filled box cannot be undone, so it is the safest choice for passwords and keys.
2. **Pixelate it.** Large pixel blocks work well for names, numbers and faces, and they make it obvious that something was hidden on purpose.
3. **Crop it out.** If the sensitive part is at the edge of the image, cropping removes it entirely.

One rule of thumb: never rely on a light blur for secrets such as keys or passwords. Cover those completely, or crop them out.

[Ledge](/en/) is a free Mac menu bar app that keeps your latest screenshots on a shelf at the top of your screen, so you do not have to go looking for them on the Desktop. Combining it with the steps above looks like this:

Everything happens on your Mac. Ledge has no account and makes no network requests, so the screenshot you are about to redact never goes anywhere until you send it yourself.

## Quick checklist

- Zoom out and look at the whole screenshot once more.
- Hide secrets with a solid cover, not a light blur.
- Check the menu bar, tabs and notifications.
- Preview the final image before sending it.
